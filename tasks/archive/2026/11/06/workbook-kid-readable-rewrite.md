# Kids' workbook: fifth-grade rewrite, unique move names, a picture-led example, and an answer key

**Completed:** 2026-11-06. The maintainer, William Emerison Six <billsix@gmail.com>, approved the
print. The work was done in two rounds. Round 1 (2026-11-05) was a rewrite. Round 2 (2026-11-06)
reopened this task after the maintainer reviewed the print. The current design is described in the
workbook bullet of `tasks/reference/architecture-overview.md`.

## Round 1 (2026-11-05): fifth-grade rewrite

`workbook/induction-workbook.tex` already taught the right method ("solve small, relabel, replay"),
but in adult language and without saying *how* to copy and swap. It was rewritten at a fifth-grade
reading level.

- Each copy row became copy boxes → a swap arrow → result boxes. That is the paper form of the
  game's Recipe | Rebinding | Rebound view (`tasks/reference/feature-spec.md` §4.8).
- Added: a cover with step pictures, both swap keys and the change-it-twice warning; a worked
  example; a grown-ups page mapping each step to `hanoi-cli` commands; cut-out discs and a peg mat.
  Text became 12pt sans-serif.
- `enumitem` was dropped because neither the sandbox nor the book image ships it.
- The four `workbook/hanoi1..4.svg` worksheets were deleted:
  - their colours didn't match the booklet and the game;
  - their boxes were coloured by the unswapped numbers;
  - their wording led to the change-it-twice trap;
  - their text had been converted to paths.
- A survey of the CLI confirmed that the workbook's keys match the game. Under `relabel 1 3 2` /
  `apply solve-(n-1)` / `relabel 1 2 3` / `1 -> 3` / `relabel 2 1 3` / `apply solve-(n-1)`, the game
  wins n=2..5 in 2^n−1 moves.

## Round 2 (2026-11-06): the maintainer's review and the fixes

Review (William Emerison Six <billsix@gmail.com>):

- The cover page was good.
- Page 2 was good, but one picture printed "4" outside its disc.
- The worked example (page 3) did not help a newcomer.
- The main problem was the "old move k" / "new move m" labels. They changed meaning from sheet to
  sheet, and "find old move 1" didn't say where to look.

What the agent found:

- **Picture bug.** Page 2's four pictures were TikZ pictures nested inside positioned nodes
  (`right=`, `below=`). The inner pictures inherited the positioning, so every disc number shifted.
  In picture B every number dropped one disc.
- **Worked example.** It had no discs, started at 3 rows per step, and used the old/new labels.
- **One move, two names.** Sheet 3's "new move 5" was Sheet 4's "old move 5".
- **Three places to look.** A sheet's moves are written in its Step A grid, its Step B box and its
  Step C grid, and the old wording didn't say which.
- **Fixed text that didn't fit.** Sheet 2 said "then old move 2, then old move 3", but it has one row.

Decisions (William Emerison Six <billsix@gmail.com>, 2026-11-06):

- Every move has one name, written out in full: "Sheet N, move K".
- Page 3 became a picture-led walkthrough of making Sheet 2 from Sheet 1.
- A full answer key, using the same names, goes on the last pages, and the booklet mentions it at
  the start.
- No per-sheet fill-in answer list. It would add one more copy step, and the move names already say
  where each move is.

What was built:

- **Move names.** `\mv{N}{K}` is used in every row label, instruction, example and box heading.
  Column headings are "COPY the move from Sheet N−1" and "SWAP and WRITE the move for Sheet N".
- **Box labels.** Each box has a small "from"/"to" tag inside its corner (`\cellbox`), so the row
  pitch and Sheet 5's one-page 15-row grid did not change.
- **Where to look.** Each sheet from Sheet 3 on says where the previous sheet's moves are.
- **Page 2.** Its pictures are laid out in a `tabular`; a comment at `\drawpeg` warns against
  nesting. A new "Every move has a name" section was added, and "How to use" points to the answer
  key.
- **Answer key.** It sits after the grown-ups page and before the cut-out discs. Each row reads
  "copy Sheet N−1, move K: a→b, swap → Sheet N, move M: c→d", and each sheet's key starts a new page
  unless it fits. The `\keycopy`/`\keyone` rows were generated by a one-shot script, removed when
  this task was archived. The script replayed every sheet on a simulated board: every move is
  legal, and each sheet ends with all discs on peg 3 in 2^n−1 moves. Its 3-disc output equals the
  game's optimal solve.
- The page-break helper is plain TeX, because `needspace` is not assumed in the book image.

Verification: `make -C workbook` exited 0 with no errors and no overfull boxes, and produced 21
pages. Every page was rendered with `pdftoppm` and checked by eye.
